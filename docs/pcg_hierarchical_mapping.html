1. Executive Summary

mPower's DC/IR solve is a preconditioned conjugate gradient iteration. It looks unfamiliar because no single object is "the matrix" and no single vector is "the residual" — both are spread across partitions and never assembled. The mapping to textbook PCG is nonetheless exact, once three ideas are clear:

  1. The unknowns are port voltages, not node voltages. CG runs only on the tearing nodes left after each partition's interior is Schur-complemented away. Interior voltages are recovered afterwards by back-substitution — CG never sees them.
  2. The vectors are duplicated, not assembled. Each partition keeps a private copy of every port it touches, so a node shared by three partitions occupies three slots. Assembly happens only inside the preconditioner.
  3. Exactness and approximation live in different places. The operator is exact; the preconditioner is approximate. That split is the whole design: an exact operator makes CG converge to the right answer, a cheap approximate preconditioner makes it converge fast.

2. Textbook PCG

The standard algorithm for \(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{b}\), with \(\mathbf{A}\) symmetric positive definite and preconditioner \(\mathbf{M}\approx\mathbf{A}\):

1 \(\mathbf{u}_0\) given, \(\quad \mathbf{p}_0=\mathbf{0}, \quad \rho_{0}=1\)
2 \(\mathbf{r}_0=\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{u}_0\)   initial residual
3 for \(k=1,2,3,\dots\)
4 \(\mathbf{z}_k=\mathbf{M}^{-1}\mathbf{r}_{k-1}\)   preconditioner solve
5 \(\rho_k=\mathbf{r}_{k-1}^{T}\mathbf{z}_k\)   inner product
6 \(\beta_k=\rho_k/\rho_{k-1}\)
7 \(\mathbf{p}_k=\mathbf{z}_k+\beta_k\,\mathbf{p}_{k-1}\)   search direction
8 \(\mathbf{q}_k=\mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{p}_k\)   matrix–vector product
9 \(\alpha_k=\rho_k/(\mathbf{p}_k^{T}\mathbf{q}_k)\)   inner product
10 \(\mathbf{u}_k=\mathbf{u}_{k-1}+\alpha_k\,\mathbf{p}_k\)
11 \(\mathbf{r}_k=\mathbf{r}_{k-1}-\alpha_k\,\mathbf{q}_k\)
12 if converged break
13 end

Two structural properties matter, because mPower exploits both:

  • The matrices are never needed explicitly. Only three things touch \(\mathbf{A}\) and \(\mathbf{M}\): one mat-vec (line 8), one preconditioner solve (line 4), and inner products (lines 5, 9). Anything that can apply \(\mathbf{A}\) to a vector will do. This is what permits a matrix-free, partition-distributed implementation.
  • The two highlighted lines have opposite requirements. \(\mathbf{A}\) must be applied exactly — an inconsistent operator breaks the Krylov relations and CG converges to the wrong answer, or not at all. \(\mathbf{M}^{-1}\) may be any SPD approximation; its quality affects only the iteration count. mPower places its approximation entirely on the \(\mathbf{M}^{-1}\) side.

3. The Hierarchical PCG System in mPower

Three questions answer themselves in order: which system is being solved, how its vectors are stored, and where the approximation sits. Everything else in the solver follows from these.

3.1 The system CG solves: a reduced port system

Start from the circuit system \(\mathbf{G}\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{b}\) and split each partition \(p\) into its interior and its ports:

\[ \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{A}_p & \mathbf{B}_p \\ \mathbf{B}_p^T & \mathbf{D}_p \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{v}_p^{(\text{int})} \\ \mathbf{v}_p^{(\text{port})} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{b}_p^{(\text{int})} \\ \mathbf{b}_p^{(\text{port})} \end{bmatrix} \]

Eliminating the interior gives the partition Schur complement and compressed right-hand side:

\[ \mathbf{S}_p=\mathbf{D}_p-\mathbf{B}_p^T\mathbf{A}_p^{-1}\mathbf{B}_p, \qquad \tilde{\mathbf{b}}_p=\mathbf{b}_p^{(\text{port})}-\mathbf{B}_p^T\mathbf{A}_p^{-1}\mathbf{b}_p^{(\text{int})} \]

With \(\mathbf{R}_p\) the boolean map from global tearing nodes to partition \(p\)'s local port ordering, the exact reduced system is:

\[ \boxed{\;\hat{\mathbf{S}}\,\mathbf{u}=\hat{\mathbf{b}}, \qquad \hat{\mathbf{S}}=\sum_p \mathbf{R}_p^T\mathbf{S}_p\mathbf{R}_p, \qquad \hat{\mathbf{b}}=\sum_p \mathbf{R}_p^T\tilde{\mathbf{b}}_p\;} \]

This is the system CG solves. It is SPD whenever the circuit is, since it is a Schur complement of an SPD matrix. Afterwards each partition recovers its own interior independently: \(\mathbf{v}_p^{(\text{int})}=\mathbf{A}_p^{-1}(\mathbf{b}_p^{(\text{int})}-\mathbf{B}_p\mathbf{v}_p^{(\text{port})})\).

3.2 How the vectors are stored: the duplicated port space

Here mPower departs from the textbook picture. It never builds \(\hat{\mathbf{S}}\), and its CG vectors are not of length \(n_{\text{tnode}}\). Instead every partition holds private copies of its own ports, so shared nodes are stored more than once:

Stacking the \(\mathbf{R}_p\) into \(\mathbf{R}\) and the \(\mathbf{S}_p\) into a block-diagonal operator gives the identity that makes the whole scheme valid:

\[ \mathbf{R}^{T}\,\underbrace{\operatorname{blockdiag}(\mathbf{S}_0,\mathbf{S}_1,\dots)}_{\textstyle \mathbf{A}_{\text{dup}}}\,\mathbf{R} \;=\;\sum_p \mathbf{R}_p^T\mathbf{S}_p\mathbf{R}_p\;=\;\hat{\mathbf{S}} \]

Working in the duplicated space with a block-diagonal operator is therefore equivalent to working in the assembled space with \(\hat{\mathbf{S}}\).

Duplication has one consequence worth stating up front, because the inner products depend on it: the CG vectors are not all duplicated in the same way. Voltage-like vectors (\(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{p},\mathbf{z}\)) hold the same value in every copy; current-like vectors (\(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{q}\)) hold a share in each copy, and only their sum is meaningful. Pairing one of each is what lets the dot products be computed locally and simply added up. Appendix A.1 works this through.

3.3 Where the approximation sits: exact operator, approximate preconditioner

This is the single most important thing to understand about the solver, and the easiest to get backwards.

Figure 3 — the exact/approximate split
EXACT OPERATOR   \(\mathbf{A}_{\text{dup}} = \operatorname{blockdiag}(\mathbf{S}_p)\)
  • Each \(\mathbf{S}_p\) is the dense partition Schur complement.
  • With EXACT_PART_MTX = true (default), nothing is ever dropped or lumped from it.
  • ⟹ \(\mathbf{R}^{T}\mathbf{A}_{\text{dup}}\mathbf{R} = \hat{\mathbf{S}}\) exactly, to round-off.
  • ⟹ CG converges to the true solution.
APPROXIMATE PRECONDITIONER   \(\mathbf{M}^{-1}\) — a two-stage cascade
  • The hierarchical direct solve, built on intm and intm2 matrices that are sparsified: LEVEL1_SCHUR_TOL = 1e-4 (intm), LEVEL2_SCHUR_TOL = 1e-4 (intm2). Small off-diagonals are lumped onto the diagonal rather than dropped (mechanism detail in §7.4).
  • ⟹ affects the iteration count only — never the answer.

The preconditioner is a cascade of two restrictions with a Schur elimination between them — it descends through two different maps, and never forms an \(n_{\text{tnode}}\times n_{\text{tnode}}\) inverse at all:

\[ \mathbf{M}^{-1}\;=\; \mathbf{R}_1\,\Bigl[\,\mathcal{E}^{-1}\circ\bigl(\mathbf{R}_2\,\tilde{\mathbf{S}}_{GG}^{-1}\,\mathbf{R}_2^{T}\bigr)\circ\mathcal{E}\,\Bigr]\,\mathbf{R}_1^{T} \]
SymbolMapOn the 4×4 grid example
\(\mathbf{R}_1\) scatter: intm tearing nodes → partition ports
(\(\mathbf{R}_1^T\) is the gather; both read simPart::port_map)		 \(\mathbf{R}_1^T\) gathers 11 partition-port slots → 7 intm tearing slots; \(\mathbf{R}_1\) scatters back. Note 7, not 5: n5 and n9 belong to both intms, so the intm level is still duplicated.
\(\mathcal{E}\) eliminate LPORTs (Schur step, A.2) Within each intm, removes the LPORT block, leaving only GPORTs: intm 0 drops n4, intm 1 drops n6, n7.
\(\mathbf{R}_2\) scatter: intm2 nodes → intm GPORTs
(\(\mathbf{R}_2^T\) is the gather; both read simIntm::port_map)		 \(\mathbf{R}_2^T\) gathers 4 GPORT slots (2 per intm) → 2 intm2 nodes {n5, n9}; \(\mathbf{R}_2\) scatters back.
\(\tilde{\mathbf{S}}_{GG}\) the only matrix actually inverted 2×2, spanning GPORTs only — not a 5×5 inverse of \(\hat{\mathbf{S}}\).
Direction convention. As in §3.1 and Figure 2, each \(\mathbf{R}\) denotes the scatter (coarse → duplicated-fine) and each \(\mathbf{R}^T\) the gather. Read the formula right-to-left: the residual is gathered down (\(\mathbf{R}_1^T\), then \(\mathcal{E}\), then \(\mathbf{R}_2^T\)), solved at the top, and scattered back up — matching the code map in A.3. Do not read the operator direction off the port_map arrays: they store local → global indices (port_map[local] = global), and that one array implements both operators — the scatter reads v[i] = u[port_map[i]], the gather accumulates b[port_map[i]] += r[i].

Two consequences follow:

  • The LPORTs never reach the top level. They are eliminated by \(\mathcal{E}\) on the way down and recovered by \(\mathcal{E}^{-1}\) on the way back up, entirely inside their own intm. Only GPORTs pass through \(\mathbf{R}_2\).
  • Duplication is removed in two stages, not one. 11 → 7 → 2 in this example. The intermediate space is still duplicated across intms, which is exactly why a second gather/scatter level exists.

Sparsification, where it does apply, does not simply discard a small entry — it lumps it onto the two diagonal entries of its row and column. That preserves row sums, so the sparsified matrix stays a legitimate conductance matrix and remains SPD. This matters: CG requires an SPD preconditioner, so an SPD-preserving approximation is what lets the tolerance be loosened for speed without breaking the iteration.

The design in one sentence. Exact operator ⟹ CG converges to the right answer; cheap approximate preconditioner ⟹ CG converges quickly. Loosening the sparsification tolerances trades iteration count against per-iteration cost and memory, and cannot change the converged result.
If EXACT_PART_MTX were set to false, the partition level would switch to a sparse, thresholded Schur matrix. The CG operator would itself become approximate and this guarantee would be lost.

Putting the pieces together, one CG iteration has exactly three kinds of work, with very different costs:

Each partition's port list mixes GPORTs (shared between intms, resolved at the top level) and LPORTs (interior to one intm). The sweep goes down then up: LPORTs are eliminated into the GPORT system on the way down, the small GPORT system is solved at the top, then LPORTs are recovered on the way back up. Only after that back-substitution does every entry of \(\mathbf{z}_p\) have a value.

Each partition contributes a partial sum, reduced across threads and MPI ranks. Two reductions per iteration is the theoretical minimum for CG.

Reading the figure:

  • ① is the expensive, coupled step — and it is the approximate one. Everything that makes partitions talk to each other is confined here.
  • ② is cheap and perfectly parallel — and it is the exact one. No partition needs anything from another.
  • ③ is two scalars. Two reductions per iteration is the theoretical minimum for CG, so the hierarchical structure adds no synchronisation beyond what the algorithm inherently requires.
Parallelism. Within a machine, partitions run concurrently on a thread pool with a barrier between stages; each touches only its own slices, so no locking is needed. Across MPI ranks, each rank owns a contiguous block of intms, and only the two scalars in ③ plus the gather/scatter in ① cross rank boundaries.

6. Convergence Criteria

The stopping rule is richer than a plain residual threshold. In words, CG stops when:

  1. The relative residual is below tolerance — measured on the gathered residual \(\|\mathbf{R}^T\mathbf{r}\|/\|\hat{\mathbf{b}}\|\), i.e. the residual of the assembled system \(\hat{\mathbf{S}}\mathbf{u}=\hat{\mathbf{b}}\). This is the correct quantity to test, not the duplicated-space norm.
  2. and it has improved by at least a half-decade relative to the first iteration (\(\text{resid}\le\text{resid}_1/\sqrt{10}\)) — preventing a premature exit when the starting residual happened to be small already.
  3. and a minimum iteration count has been passed — bypassed only when the absolute residual is already below \(10^{-15}\).

Two additional exits: an absolute-tolerance mode used for certain analyses, and a divergence guard that aborts if the residual more than doubles — which an approximate preconditioner plus finite precision can occasionally cause.

7. Asymmetric Systems: the is_pkg Path

Sections 1–6 describe the default SPD flow. This section covers the CPM asymmetric path with EXACT_PART_MTX = true, sparsification_method = 1, and KLU for general sparse factorizations. It describes what the implementation does; it does not imply that classical PCG is mathematically valid for a general matrix.

CPM mode selection. HIGH_ORDER_CPM=1 selects the symmetric CPM reference (FORCE_SYMMETRIC_VSRC remains true); HIGH_ORDER_CPM=2 selects the directed CPM path (FORCE_SYMMETRIC_VSRC=false). ENABLE_ASYM_SPARSIFICATION is an internal derived control, not a user or Tcl option: it follows !FORCE_SYMMETRIC_VSRC.

The switch is the per-matrix flag mtxPart::is_pkg (mtx/mtxPart.h:40). Despite its name, the flag means “use general-matrix storage and factorization” rather than only “this is a package.”

7.2 The structural consequence: a fourth block

For a genuinely general block matrix, the lower-left block is independent of the upper-right block:

\[ \begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{A}&\mathbf{B}\\\mathbf{C}&\mathbf{D}\end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{x}_I\\\mathbf{x}_P\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{b}_I\\\mathbf{b}_P\end{bmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{S}=\mathbf{D}-\mathbf{C}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{B}, \qquad \tilde{\mathbf{b}}_P=\mathbf{b}_P-\mathbf{C}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{b}_I. \]

mPower stores \(\mathbf{B}\) in cmtx. Its explicit \(\mathbf{C}\) block is bmtx, described in the header as “balancing current equations” (mtxPart.h:49). The important detail is that bmtx is allocated only for the intm matrix, not for bottom-level partitions.

Naming trap. In the intm branch of schur_complement_pkg, the variable holding bmtx is named mtx2_T (mtx/mtxOpts.cc:363-374). No transpose is performed there: the object already stores \(\mathbf{C}\). In the dense partition branch, however, the code really does transpose cmtx. The same function therefore means two different things depending on which output pointer is present.

7.3 How the unchanged CG driver consumes it

The matrix setup changes, but the iterative driver does not. In the default single-process path, is_pkg propagates from an affected partition to its containing intm; if any intm is affected, intm2 also uses general storage. The affected hierarchy path therefore uses KLU LU, while unaffected sibling intms may remain symmetric and use CHOLMOD. When FORCE_SYMMETRIC_VSRC = false, every partition and intm is marked general.

Figure 6 — where asymmetry lives in the hierarchy
LEVEL MATRIX HANDLED BY USED BY CG AS ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── partition mtx (general interior) KLU LU setup dense surrogate Schur full dense mat-vec ▶ OPERATOR A ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── affected intm + bmtx KLU LU ┐ intm (unaffected siblings may remain symmetric) │ intm2 general if any intm is affected; KLU LU ┘ PRECOND M⁻¹ ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── CG's two matrix touches, neither of which has an asymmetric branch: q_p = S_p · p_p a dense mat-vec (mtxPart::mult_schur) z = M⁻¹ r a black-box solve (simIntm2::precond_solve)

CG itself never inspects a matrix. Its two matrix touches are mult_schur (mtxPart.cc:1043), a dense mat-vec against the stored \(\mathbf{S}_p\), and precond_solve, a black-box hierarchical solve. Neither has an asymmetric branch, and neither needs a storage-specific one: dsMatrix::mult and spMatrix dispatch internally on their symmetry flags. The gather/scatter primitives are unchanged because they are pure vector operations.

A dsMatrix constructed with sym=false allocates full \(n^2\) storage rather than packed triangular (dsMatrix.h:126-131), and its mult takes the general branch rather than the symmetric one (dsMatrix.h:197-214). spMatrix::mult does the same (spMatrix.cc). The stored general matrix is therefore multiplied as stored. Full dense storage uses nearly twice the memory of packed symmetric storage, while the dense mat-vec arithmetic remains of the same \(O(n^2)\) order and similar operation count.

The Krylov method does not change, so classical PCG theory no longer applies. There is one distributed iterative driver in the codebase, solve_cg_dist (mtx/iterSolve.cc:110), and it always uses the ordinary CG recurrences \(\rho=\mathbf{r}^{T}\mathbf{z}\), \(\beta=\rho_k/\rho_{k-1}\), and \(\alpha=\rho/(\mathbf{p}^{T}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{p})\). Neither is_pkg nor matrix symmetry selects a nonsymmetric Krylov method.

Classical CG is derived under the assumption that \(\mathbf{A}\) is symmetric positive definite: the three-term recurrence that makes it cheap relies on \(\mathbf{A}\)-orthogonality, which is not an inner product when \(\mathbf{A}\neq\mathbf{A}^{T}\). A general LU preconditioner is not guaranteed SPD either. The implementation may converge, stall, or diverge; its denominator, non-finite-value, iteration-limit, and residual-growth checks detect some breakdowns but do not supply a convergence guarantee.
Proposed remedy. A transpose-free nonsymmetric Krylov driver — preconditioned BiCGSTAB — can run on this same infrastructure with the operator, preconditioner, and gather/scatter primitives unchanged, replacing only the recurrence. The full mapping, including the one new communication primitive it needs, is developed in bicgstab_hierarchical_mapping.html.
Meaning of “exact operator” in this path. The mat-vec applies the stored dense partition Schur without sparsifying it. It is exact for that stored surrogate operator, not necessarily for the original fully general partition matrix, because the bottom-level reduction substitutes \(\mathbf{B}^{T}\) for an independently stamped \(\mathbf{C}\).

Directed CPM result. Method 1 now prunes directed off-diagonals at both upper levels. At level 2, the local intm contributions are assembled into the aggregate intm2 matrix before the post-assembly sweep. In distributed MUMPS operation, the diagonal reference is globally reduced before pruning and the before/after/candidate/drop/retain counts are reduced and reported once by rank 0; the collected serial path reports the assembled aggregate directly. The stored dense partition operator is unchanged.

For directed method 1, every diagonal is retained. Each stored off-diagonal \(A_{r,c}\) is tested independently using \(|A_{r,c}| < \tau|A_{r,r}|\) and \(|A_{r,c}| < \tau|A_{c,c}|\). A dropped entry is lumped onto both endpoint diagonals, and the reverse entry is never synthesized. Assembly adapts its traversal to general storage; the full square is walked rather than one triangle, and the mirror write is suppressed. This preserves directed entries instead of silently symmetrizing them.

Diagonal lumping has no general asymmetric guarantee. For a directed off-diagonal \(d=A_{ji}\), the code removes \(d\) and adds it to both endpoint diagonals. Unlike the symmetric conductance case, this does not generally preserve every row sum and does not preserve SPD, because a general matrix was not SPD to begin with. It is a preconditioner heuristic in this path, not a structure-preserving transformation.
Summary. In the default serial flow, is_pkg selects general storage and KLU LU, preserves an explicit lower-left bmtx at the intm level, and method-1 directed sparsification is applied at both intm and aggregate intm2. Bottom-level partitions are different: they do not retain bmtx, so their dense operator uses the surrogate reduction \(\mathbf{D}-\mathbf{B}^{T}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{B}\). None of these choices changes the CG recurrence. The implementation therefore supports applying and preconditioning stored general matrices, but it does not provide the convergence guarantees of a nonsymmetric Krylov solver.

8. Appendix

The detail behind three points asserted in Section 3 and Section 5, for readers who need to go deeper.

A.1 Vector typing in the duplicated space

Think of the difference physically. A voltage at a shared node is one number that every partition must agree on. A current into a shared node is a sum: each partition delivers part of it, and only the total is meaningful. Duplicated storage represents these two situations differently:

Figure A1 — the two vector types at a node shared by P0, P1, P2
CONSISTENT ("shared value" — like a voltage) produced by SCATTER P0: 0.7 P1: 0.7 P2: 0.7 └──────────────┴──────────────┘ all equal true value of the node = 0.7 (read any one slot) DISTRIBUTED ("split contribution" — like a current) produced locally P0: 0.2 P1: 0.5 P2: 0.3 └──────────────┴──────────────┘ sum true value of the node = 1.0 (must gather to read it)
How each CG vector is typed, and why:
  • \(\mathbf{p}\) and \(\mathbf{u}\) (search direction, iterate) — consistent. They are voltage-like. \(\mathbf{u}\) must be, because a node has one voltage.
  • \(\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{M}^{-1}\mathbf{r}\) — consistent, and necessarily so: the preconditioner ends with a scatter, which copies one solved value into every owner.
  • \(\mathbf{r}\) and \(\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{p}\) — distributed. They are current-like. Each partition computes only its own contribution (\(\mathbf{q}_p=\mathbf{S}_p\mathbf{p}_p\) uses partition \(p\)'s conductances alone), so no single slot holds the node's true residual; only the sum over owners does.

The typing is not a convention someone chose — it falls out of how each vector is produced. A scatter yields consistent vectors; a purely local computation yields distributed ones. And the two types are preserved by the algorithm: line 7 builds \(\mathbf{p}\) from \(\mathbf{z}\) and \(\mathbf{p}\) (both consistent, so \(\mathbf{p}\) stays consistent); line 11 updates \(\mathbf{r}\) using \(\mathbf{q}\) (both distributed, so \(\mathbf{r}\) stays distributed). The types never leak across.

Why this makes the dot products come out right

Both CG dot products pair a distributed vector with a consistent one — \(\mathbf{r}^T\mathbf{z}\) and \(\mathbf{p}^T\mathbf{q}\) — and for that pairing, naively summing the local dot products gives the true assembled answer, with no weighting and no correction:

\[ \sum_p \mathbf{r}_p^{T}\mathbf{z}_p \;=\;(\mathbf{R}^{T}\mathbf{r})^{T}\,\mathbf{z}_{\text{asm}} \;=\;\hat{\mathbf{r}}^{T}\hat{\mathbf{z}} \]

because the consistent vector contributes the same value \(\hat z\) at every copy, so summing over copies performs the gather \(\mathbf{R}^T\) on the distributed one for free.

This is why rdotz and zAp_zdotp can simply accumulate w += z[i]*r[i] over each partition's private slice and add up the partials. No partition needs to know that a node is shared, or with how many others.

What goes wrong if the types are mixed. Pair two consistent vectors and every shared node is counted once per owner, inflating the dot product by its multiplicity. On the 4×4 grid — where n5 has 3 owners and n4, n6, n7, n9 have 2 each — a dot product that should be 16 evaluates to 28. Since \(\rho\) and \(\mathbf{p}^T\mathbf{q}\) set \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\), corrupted scalars break the conjugacy relations and CG stalls or diverges. Pairing two distributed vectors is wrong in the opposite direction, under-counting the cross terms between partitions.
The rule in one line: every CG inner product must pair one distributed vector with one consistent vector. mPower satisfies this automatically because \(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{q}\) are always distributed and \(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{p},\mathbf{u}\) are always consistent. ✓

A.2 "Eliminate LPORTs" — the intm-level Schur step

It is one Schur-complement step, the same algebra used to remove partition interiors in §3.1, applied one level up. Order each intm's tearing nodes as LPORT block \(L\) then GPORT block \(G\):

\[ \begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{A}_{LL} & \mathbf{B}_{LG}\\ \mathbf{B}_{LG}^{T} & \mathbf{D}_{GG}\end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{u}_L\\ \mathbf{u}_G\end{bmatrix}= \begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{b}_L\\ \mathbf{b}_G\end{bmatrix} \]
  • Down (eliminate). Factor \(\mathbf{A}_{LL}\) and form the reduced GPORT system \(\bigl(\mathbf{D}_{GG}-\mathbf{B}_{LG}^{T}\mathbf{A}_{LL}^{-1}\mathbf{B}_{LG}\bigr)\mathbf{u}_G = \mathbf{b}_G-\mathbf{B}_{LG}^{T}\mathbf{A}_{LL}^{-1}\mathbf{b}_L\). The LPORT unknowns are now gone; only their effect on the GPORTs remains. This reduced matrix is what gets assembled into intm2 — and what the 1e-4 sparsification is applied to.
  • Up (back-substitute). Once the top level returns \(\mathbf{u}_G\), recover the LPORTs with \(\mathbf{u}_L=\mathbf{A}_{LL}^{-1}\bigl(\mathbf{b}_L-\mathbf{B}_{LG}\mathbf{u}_G\bigr)\), reusing the factorization from the downward pass.
The elimination is only half a solve; without the upward pass the LPORT entries of \(\mathbf{z}\) would be undefined and \(\mathbf{M}^{-1}\) would not be a solve at all.

A.4 Line-by-line equivalence: the duplicated loop is PCG on \(\hat{\mathbf{S}}\)

Section 3.2's identity \(\hat{\mathbf{S}}=\mathbf{R}^{T}\mathbf{A}_{\text{dup}}\mathbf{R}\) is never evaluated as a matrix product — the code holds only its three factors separately (\(\mathbf{R}\) = update_port scatter, \(\mathbf{R}^{T}\) = fill_intm_rhs gather, \(\mathbf{A}_{\text{dup}}\) = per-partition mult_schur). What the identity provides is an invariant: every line of the Section 4 loop, read through the gather, is exactly the corresponding textbook line on the assembled system. The dictionary is the typing of A.1: a consistent vector stores \(\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{R}\hat{\mathbf{x}}\) (any copy reads back \(\hat{\mathbf{x}}\)); a distributed vector stores shares whose gather \(\hat{\mathbf{r}}=\mathbf{R}^{T}\mathbf{r}\) is the assembled value.

The identity therefore fires in exactly three disguises — the initial residual, the gathered meaning of the mat-vec, and the curvature scalar — always sandwiched between consistent vectors: \((\mathbf{R}\hat{\mathbf{x}})^{T}\mathbf{A}_{\text{dup}}(\mathbf{R}\hat{\mathbf{y}}) =\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{T}\hat{\mathbf{S}}\hat{\mathbf{y}}\). That sandwich is why blockwise local work plus scalar sums suffices. Note in particular that \(\mathbf{q}\) is never gathered: its only consumers are the dot product (where the consistent partner performs the gather implicitly) and the \(\mathbf{r}\) update (where distributed stays distributed).

The invariant, once. If at the top of an iteration \(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{p}\) are consistent (\(\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{R}\hat{\mathbf{u}}\), \(\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{R}\hat{\mathbf{p}}\)) and \(\mathbf{R}^{T}\mathbf{r}=\hat{\mathbf{r}}\), then after one pass of the Section 4 loop the same relations hold for the textbook-updated \(\hat{\mathbf{u}},\hat{\mathbf{p}},\hat{\mathbf{r}}\), and every scalar (\(\rho,\beta,\alpha\)) mPower computes equals its textbook value. By induction the duplicated loop is PCG on \(\hat{\mathbf{S}}=\mathbf{R}^{T}\mathbf{A}_{\text{dup}}\mathbf{R}\) — with \(\hat{\mathbf{S}}\) existing only as a theorem about the code, never as a data structure. ✓
Why consistency is load-bearing. Were \(\mathbf{p}\) not consistent, mult_schur would apply each \(\mathbf{S}_p\) to a different per-copy value, and \(\mathbf{R}^{T}\mathbf{q}\) would gather to something that is not \(\hat{\mathbf{S}}\hat{\mathbf{p}}\) — indeed not the product of any matrix with any vector. The typing discipline of A.1 is what keeps the sandwich identity applicable at every iteration.
