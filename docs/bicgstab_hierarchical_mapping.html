1. Executive Summary

Companion §7 documents the asymmetric path honestly: when is_pkg marks the hierarchy general (package/controlled-source stamps, or the flipped-current voltage-source stamp), the stored matrices are genuinely nonsymmetric, KLU replaces CHOLMOD — and the driver still runs classical CG, whose three-term recurrence is derived from a symmetry that no longer exists. The implementation may converge, stall, or diverge, and its guards only detect some of the failures after the fact.

This document maps preconditioned BiCGSTAB — a Krylov method derived for general nonsingular matrices — onto the same hierarchical infrastructure. Three facts make the extension small:

  1. BiCGSTAB is transpose-free. It applies only \(\mathbf{A}\) and \(\mathbf{M}^{-1}\), never \(\mathbf{A}^{T}\) or \(\mathbf{M}^{-T}\). The two matrix touches the infrastructure already provides — the stored dense partition Schur mat-vec and the black-box hierarchical solve — are exactly the two touches BiCGSTAB needs. No new matrix kernel is required.
  2. The duplicated-space typing discipline carries over, with one new rule. The operator still converts consistent → distributed and the preconditioner distributed → consistent, exactly as in CG. But BiCGSTAB's scalars pair two distributed vectors (\(\mathbf{t}^{T}\mathbf{s}\), \(\mathbf{t}^{T}\mathbf{t}\)), which the CG local-dot trick cannot evaluate. Section 4 shows the two-part fix: store the shadow residual consistently, and evaluate the remaining pairs on gathered (assembled) slices.
  3. Everything new lives in the driver. One new loop (solve_bicgstab_dist) beside solve_cg_dist, one new communication primitive composed from four existing calls, four extra per-partition vectors. The matrices, factorizations, sparsification, gather/scatter machinery, and MPI layout are untouched.

With the schedule of Section 7, the loop keeps CG's two scalar reductions per iteration; the extra cost is one preconditioner solve, one mat-vec, and one gather round per iteration — the unavoidable price of a method that is actually valid for the matrix being solved.

2. Why BiCGSTAB (and not BiCG, QMR, CGS, or GMRES)

The decisive constraint is the preconditioner transpose. The hierarchical solve is a cascade — gather, eliminate LPORTs, top-level KLU solve, back-substitute, scatter. Applying \(\mathbf{M}^{-T}\) would require running that cascade mirrored: transposed KLU solves exist, but the traversal itself (scatter and gather swap roles, the elimination \(\mathcal{E}\) transposes) would be a second, parallel code path through simIntm2 / simIntm / simPart. Avoiding that is worth more than any per-iteration nicety.

MethodNeeds \(\mathbf{A}^{T}\), \(\mathbf{M}^{-T}\)?Memory / communicationVerdict here
BiCG Yes — both, every iteration Like CG ×2 Rejected: mirrored cascade required.
QMR Yes (classical form) Like BiCG, smoother residuals Rejected for the same reason.
CGS No Like BiCGSTAB Rejected: squares the BiCG polynomial — residuals so erratic that BiCGSTAB was invented to replace it.
GMRES(m) No Stores \(m\) duplicated-space basis vectors; orthogonalisation costs \(O(m)\) reductions per iteration (or one, with stability risk) Robust fallback if BiCGSTAB stagnates, but breaks the fixed-memory, two-reduction structure the driver is built around.
BiCGSTAB No Fixed: 8 per-partition vectors, 2 reductions/iteration (Section 7) Selected. Transpose-free, fixed memory, drops into the existing driver pattern.
Future options. BiCGSTAB's degree-1 stabilisation step is known to weaken on spectra with large imaginary parts — plausible for package/controlled-source systems. If that is observed, BiCGSTAB(ℓ) (ℓ = 2) and IDR(s) are the standard transpose-free escalations; both reuse this document's typing and communication analysis unchanged. They are out of scope here.

3. Textbook Preconditioned BiCGSTAB

The standard algorithm (van der Vorst 1992; Barrett et al., Templates, 1994) for \(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{b}\) with \(\mathbf{A}\) nonsingular — no symmetry assumed — and preconditioner \(\mathbf{M}\approx\mathbf{A}\), applied on the right (the reason for that choice follows the listing):

1 \(\mathbf{u}_0\) given, \(\quad \hat{\mathbf{r}}_0\) chosen, \(\quad \mathbf{p}_0=\mathbf{v}_0=\mathbf{0}, \quad \rho_0=\alpha=\omega_0=1\)
2 \(\mathbf{r}_0=\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{u}_0\)   initial residual
3 for \(k=1,2,3,\dots\)
4 \(\rho_k=\hat{\mathbf{r}}_0^{T}\,\mathbf{r}_{k-1}\)   shadow inner product — breakdown if zero
5 \(\beta_k=(\rho_k/\rho_{k-1})\,(\alpha/\omega_{k-1})\)   α carried over from the previous iteration
6 \(\mathbf{p}_k=\mathbf{r}_{k-1}+\beta_k\,(\mathbf{p}_{k-1}-\omega_{k-1}\,\mathbf{v}_{k-1})\)
7 \(\hat{\mathbf{p}}=\mathbf{M}^{-1}\mathbf{p}_k\)   preconditioner solve #1
8 \(\mathbf{v}_k=\mathbf{A}\,\hat{\mathbf{p}}\)   mat-vec #1
9 \(\alpha=\rho_k\,/\,(\hat{\mathbf{r}}_0^{T}\mathbf{v}_k)\)   breakdown if denominator zero
10 \(\mathbf{s}=\mathbf{r}_{k-1}-\alpha\,\mathbf{v}_k\)
11 if \(\|\mathbf{s}\|\) small: \(\;\mathbf{u}_k=\mathbf{u}_{k-1}+\alpha\,\hat{\mathbf{p}}\), break   half-step exit
12 \(\hat{\mathbf{s}}=\mathbf{M}^{-1}\mathbf{s}\)   preconditioner solve #2
13 \(\mathbf{t}=\mathbf{A}\,\hat{\mathbf{s}}\)   mat-vec #2
14 \(\omega_k=(\mathbf{t}^{T}\mathbf{s})\,/\,(\mathbf{t}^{T}\mathbf{t})\)   residual minimisation — breakdown if zero
15 \(\mathbf{u}_k=\mathbf{u}_{k-1}+\alpha\,\hat{\mathbf{p}}+\omega_k\,\hat{\mathbf{s}}\)
16 \(\mathbf{r}_k=\mathbf{s}-\omega_k\,\mathbf{t}\)
17 if converged break
18 end

Structural notes, in the same spirit as companion §2:

  • Two preconditioner solves and two mat-vecs per iteration — one BiCGSTAB iteration is two CG-like half-steps. Per-iteration comparisons against CG should be read per mat-vec.
  • Right preconditioning is deliberate. \(\mathbf{r}_k\) remains the true residual \(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{u}_k\) of the unpreconditioned system, so the stopping test keeps exactly the meaning it has in companion §6 (\(\|\mathbf{R}^T\mathbf{r}\|/\|\hat{\mathbf{b}}\|\)), and \(\mathbf{M}^{-1}\) is consumed as the same black box the CG driver already calls (distributed vector in, consistent vector out). Left preconditioning would change both.
  • The shadow residual \(\hat{\mathbf{r}}_0\) is a free parameter. Any vector with \(\hat{\mathbf{r}}_0^{T}\mathbf{r}_0\neq 0\) is admissible; the textbook default is \(\hat{\mathbf{r}}_0=\mathbf{r}_0\). Section 4 exploits this freedom — the choice made there is what keeps the distributed dot products cheap.
  • No SPD requirement anywhere. Neither \(\mathbf{A}\) nor \(\mathbf{M}\) needs to be symmetric or definite — only nonsingular. Companion §7.4's warning that directed diagonal lumping does not preserve SPD therefore stops being a validity concern and becomes purely a quality concern (Section 9).
  • Initialisation convention: with \(\mathbf{p}_0=\mathbf{v}_0=\mathbf{0}\) and \(\rho_0=\alpha=\omega_0=1\), the first pass through lines 4–6 is well-defined and reduces to \(\mathbf{p}_1=\mathbf{r}_0\) — the same guard-value convention as the (corrected) CG listing in companion §2.

3.1 The system being solved is unchanged

Everything from companion §3 carries over verbatim: CG and BiCGSTAB solve the same reduced tearing-node system

\[ \hat{\mathbf{S}}\,\mathbf{u}=\hat{\mathbf{b}}, \qquad \hat{\mathbf{S}}=\sum_p \mathbf{R}_p^T\mathbf{S}_p\mathbf{R}_p, \qquad \hat{\mathbf{b}}=\sum_p \mathbf{R}_p^T\tilde{\mathbf{b}}_p, \]

stored in the duplicated port space, with the identity \(\mathbf{R}^{T}\operatorname{blockdiag}(\mathbf{S}_p)\,\mathbf{R}=\hat{\mathbf{S}}\) doing the work. That identity is pure index algebra — it never used symmetry — so it holds equally when each \(\mathbf{S}_p\) is the general dense surrogate Schur of the is_pkg path (companion §7.2). The exact/approximate split also survives intact: the operator is the stored dense \(\mathbf{S}_p\) applied as stored (mult_schur, general dsMatrix::mult branch); the preconditioner is the hierarchical direct solve over the sparsified intm/intm2 matrices, factored by KLU in this path. What "exact operator" means here is the caveat of companion §7.3: exact for the stored surrogate system, whose bottom level substitutes \(\mathbf{B}^{T}\) for an independently stamped \(\mathbf{C}\).

4. Vector Typing: the One New Rule

Companion A.1's discipline: consistent vectors (voltage-like — every copy holds the same value; produced by a scatter) versus distributed vectors (current-like — each copy holds a share; produced by local mat-vecs), with the rule that a dot product computed as local dots plus a sum is exact precisely when it pairs one of each. Typing every BiCGSTAB vector by how it is produced:

VectorTypeWhy
\(\mathbf{u}\) (iterate)consistent Voltages. Updated only from \(\hat{\mathbf{p}},\hat{\mathbf{s}}\) (both consistent) — stays consistent.
\(\mathbf{r}\), \(\mathbf{s}\)distributed \(\tilde{\mathbf{b}}_p\) is a per-partition share; \(\mathbf{S}_p\)-products are local shares; their linear combinations stay distributed.
\(\hat{\mathbf{p}}=\mathbf{M}^{-1}\mathbf{p}\), \(\hat{\mathbf{s}}=\mathbf{M}^{-1}\mathbf{s}\) consistent The preconditioner ends with a scatter — same as CG's \(\mathbf{z}\).
\(\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{A}\hat{\mathbf{p}}\), \(\mathbf{t}=\mathbf{A}\hat{\mathbf{s}}\) distributed Computed locally as \(\mathbf{S}_p\,(\cdot)_p\) — same as CG's \(\mathbf{q}\).
\(\mathbf{p}\) (search vector)distributed Built from \(\mathbf{r}\) and \(\mathbf{v}\) (line 6) — both distributed. Unlike CG, where \(\mathbf{p}\) was consistent: in right-preconditioned BiCGSTAB the search vector lives in residual space, and it is \(\hat{\mathbf{p}}\) that plays CG-\(\mathbf{p}\)'s role.
\(\hat{\mathbf{r}}_0\) (shadow residual)consistent — by construction A design choice, not a consequence. See below; this is the load-bearing decision of the whole mapping.

The type circuit is the same alternation as CG, run twice per iteration — the operator always consumes consistent and produces distributed, the preconditioner the reverse:

4.1 The problem: BiCGSTAB pairs distributed with distributed

Now type the five scalars the loop needs:

ScalarPairingLocal dots valid?
\(\rho_k=\hat{\mathbf{r}}_0^{T}\mathbf{r}_{k-1}\) (line 4) shadow × distributed Only if \(\hat{\mathbf{r}}_0\) is consistent
\(\hat{\mathbf{r}}_0^{T}\mathbf{v}_k\) (line 9) shadow × distributed Only if \(\hat{\mathbf{r}}_0\) is consistent
\(\mathbf{t}^{T}\mathbf{s}\) (line 14) distributed × distributed Never
\(\mathbf{t}^{T}\mathbf{t}\) (line 14) distributed × distributed Never
\(\|\mathbf{s}\|,\ \|\mathbf{r}_k\|\) (lines 11, 17) distributed with itself Never
Why "never". For two distributed vectors, the naive sum \(\sum_p \mathbf{t}_p^{T}\mathbf{s}_p\) misses every cross-partition term. Concretely, at a node shared by two partitions holding shares \(0.3\) and \(0.7\) of \(\mathbf{t}\): the true assembled contribution to \(\mathbf{t}^{T}\mathbf{t}\) is \((0.3+0.7)^2 = 1.0\); the naive local sum gives \(0.3^2+0.7^2 = 0.58\). The error is the cross term \(2\cdot 0.3\cdot 0.7\), and it can carry either sign for mixed-sign shares. Since \(\omega_k\) is the ratio of two such products, corrupted values break the residual-minimisation property silently — the same class of failure companion A.1 warns about for CG, now affecting the method's core scalars rather than a hypothetical mistake.

4.2 The fix, part one: a consistent shadow residual

\(\hat{\mathbf{r}}_0\) is a free parameter. Choose it as the consistent twin of the initial residual:

\[ \hat{\mathbf{r}}_0 \;:=\; \mathbf{R}\,(\mathbf{R}^{T}\mathbf{r}_0) \qquad\text{(gather \(\mathbf{r}_0\), scatter the assembled result back — Section 5)} \]

Three properties, each earning its keep:

  • Lines 4 and 9 become local. With \(\hat{\mathbf{r}}_0\) consistent, both shadow products pair consistent × distributed, so \(\sum_p \hat{\mathbf{r}}_{0,p}^{T}\mathbf{x}_p\) is exact (companion A.1's identity, restated in A.1). Had the textbook default \(\hat{\mathbf{r}}_0=\mathbf{r}_0\) (distributed) been kept, lines 4 and 9 would each need a gather — two extra communication rounds per iteration.
  • Breakdown-hardened start. \(\rho_1=\sum_p\hat{\mathbf{r}}_{0,p}^{T}\mathbf{r}_{0,p} =(\mathbf{R}^{T}\mathbf{r}_0)^{T}(\mathbf{R}^{T}\mathbf{r}_0)=\|\mathbf{R}^{T}\mathbf{r}_0\|^2>0\) unless the start is already converged. The textbook's \(\rho_1\neq 0\) precondition is satisfied by construction, not by luck.
  • Nearly free. The setup must gather \(\mathbf{r}_0\) anyway to obtain \(\|\mathbf{R}^{T}\mathbf{r}_0\|\) (and \(\|\hat{\mathbf{b}}\|\) for the relative test); the shadow costs only the one extra scatter of that already-gathered vector, once per solve.

4.3 The fix, part two: gathered slices for the distributed pairs

\(\mathbf{t}^{T}\mathbf{s}\), \(\mathbf{t}^{T}\mathbf{t}\), and the residual norms are inner products of the assembled system — the duplicated-space values are not merely inconvenient, they are undefined as norms (companion §6 makes the same point for CG's stopping test). So evaluate them where they are defined: on the gathered vectors \(\mathbf{s}_g=\mathbf{R}^{T}\mathbf{s}\), \(\mathbf{t}_g=\mathbf{R}^{T}\mathbf{t}\).

The infrastructure makes this cheaper than it sounds, because after the two-level gather every unique tearing node has exactly one fully-summed slot:

Figure 2 — where each unique node's fully-summed slot lives (4×4 grid of the companions)
unique tearing node kind fully-summed after lives in ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── n4 LPORT R₁ᵀ (intm 0 gather) intm 0 array n6 LPORT R₁ᵀ (intm 1 gather) intm 1 array n7 LPORT R₁ᵀ (intm 1 gather) intm 1 array n5 GPORT R₂ᵀ (intm2 gather) intm2 array (rank 0) n9 GPORT R₂ᵀ (intm2 gather) intm2 array (rank 0) An LPORT is interior to one intm, so ALL of its owners are partitions of that same intm: one R₁ᵀ gather fully sums it. Only GPORT slots need the second, cross-intm gather. t_gᵀ s_g = Σ (LPORT slots, per intm) + Σ (GPORT slots, at intm2) each unique node counted exactly once

So the dot products are sums over intm-local LPORT slices plus the intm2 GPORT slice — partial sums that reduce across threads and MPI ranks exactly like the CG scalars (companion §16.7 pattern: rank-local partials, one all-reduce). Section 7 shows that \(\mathbf{s}_g\) is already computed by preconditioner solve #2's own down-sweep, so only \(\mathbf{t}\) needs a dedicated gather — one extra round per iteration, with no solve attached.

The one new rule, stated once. Consistent × distributed pairs are local (CG's rule, unchanged). Distributed × distributed pairs must be evaluated on gathered slices, where each unique node appears exactly once. BiCGSTAB needs the second rule three times per iteration (\(\mathbf{t}^{T}\mathbf{s}\), \(\mathbf{t}^{T}\mathbf{t}\), and the residual norm — the last obtained algebraically from the first two, A.2). Everything else about the duplicated space is inherited from the CG mapping verbatim.

5. The Third Primitive: assemble = scatter ∘ gather

Companion Figure 2 established the two communication primitives: gather \(\mathbf{R}^{T}\) ("+=", currents sum) and scatter \(\mathbf{R}\) ("=", voltages copy). The BiCGSTAB driver needs their composition as a first-class operation — a type converter from distributed to consistent:

\[ \operatorname{assemble}(\mathbf{x}) \;=\; \mathbf{R}\,(\mathbf{R}^{T}\mathbf{x}) \qquad\text{distributed in} \;\longrightarrow\; \text{consistent out,} \qquad \text{no solve involved.} \]
Figure 3 — assemble: the preconditioner's communication skeleton without the solve
ASSEMBLE (distributed ──▶ consistent) P0: 0.2 ╲ ╱ P0: 1.0 P1: 0.5 ──▶ gather Rᵀ: 1.0 ──▶ scatter R ──▶ P1: 1.0 P2: 0.3 ╱ ("+=") ("=") ╲ P2: 1.0 Same two-level path as M⁻¹ (partition ⇄ intm ⇄ intm2) but with the eliminate / KLU-solve / back-substitute stages skipped: what was summed on the way down is copied straight back on the way up.

It is composed entirely from the four existing calls (fill_intm_rhs → fill_intm2_rhs for the gather, update_port_intm → update_port for the scatter — companion A.3), operating on a scratch RHS/solution buffer pair instead of the preconditioner's. Its three uses:

UseWhenWhat it provides
Shadow setup Once, before the loop \(\hat{\mathbf{r}}_0=\operatorname{assemble}(\mathbf{r}_0)\); the gather half also yields \(\rho_1=\|\mathbf{R}^{T}\mathbf{r}_0\|^2\) and the normalisation constants (\(\|\hat{\mathbf{b}}\|\) — gathered in the same round when a warm start makes \(\mathbf{r}_0\neq\tilde{\mathbf{b}}\)).
Restart shadow On breakdown (Section 8) \(\hat{\mathbf{r}}_0\leftarrow\operatorname{assemble}(\mathbf{r}_k)\), which guarantees the restart \(\rho=\|\mathbf{R}^{T}\mathbf{r}_k\|^2>0\).
\(\mathbf{t}\)-gather Every iteration Gather half only: \(\mathbf{t}_g=\mathbf{R}^{T}\mathbf{t}\) on the intm/intm2 slices, dotted there against the cached \(\mathbf{s}_g\). No scatter needed — the assembled copies feed only scalars.
Not a projector — just a converter. \(\mathbf{R}\mathbf{R}^{T}\) is not idempotent (\(\mathbf{R}^{T}\mathbf{R}=\operatorname{diag}(\text{node multiplicities})\neq\mathbf{I}\)), and nothing here requires it to be. Its only job is to move a vector from distributed to consistent representation while preserving the assembled value \(\mathbf{R}^{T}\mathbf{x}\) — which is the only value a distributed vector carries.

The identical cascade as CG's Figure 4 (companion §5): gather → eliminate LPORTs → top-level solve (KLU in this path) → back-substitute → scatter. Its quality still sets the iteration count only — but note the caveat of Section 9: for a general matrix, "only the iteration count" is an empirical statement, not a theorem.

EXACT (as stored) OPERATOR   \(\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{A}\hat{\mathbf{p}}\), \(\;\mathbf{t}=\mathbf{A}\hat{\mathbf{s}}\) — unchanged, applied twice

Two dense general \(\mathbf{S}_p\) mat-vecs per iteration (mult_schur, general dsMatrix branch, full \(n^2\) storage per companion §7.3). Perfectly parallel, no communication. "Exact" retains its §7.3 meaning: exact for the stored surrogate operator.

2 REDUCTIONS + 1 GATHER ②④⑤ SCALARS — the one genuinely new pattern

Reduction #1 carries the single shadow partial \(\hat{\mathbf{r}}_0^{T}\mathbf{v}\). Reduction #2 batches four partials — \(\mathbf{t}_g^{T}\mathbf{s}_g\), \(\mathbf{t}_g^{T}\mathbf{t}_g\) (slice dots), \(\hat{\mathbf{r}}_0^{T}\mathbf{s}\), \(\hat{\mathbf{r}}_0^{T}\mathbf{t}\) (local dots) — from which \(\omega_k\), the next \(\rho_{k+1}\), and \(\|\mathbf{R}^{T}\mathbf{r}_k\|\) all follow algebraically (A.2). The gather-only round ④ has the same MPI shape as a preconditioner down-sweep (LPORT partials rank-local; GPORT shares to rank 0) with no factorization work attached.

Read per mat-vec, one BiCGSTAB iteration is two CG-like half-steps, so comparable-quality preconditioning typically yields iteration counts near half of a hypothetical valid two-sided method — the honest statement is simply that each unit of work is now spent inside a method whose recurrences are correct for the matrix. Buffer-aliasing trap — do not inherit CG's. The CG driver overlays \(\mathbf{z}\) and \(\mathbf{q}\) in one buffer (mtxPart::s2), safe only by strict consumption order (companion A.3). The corresponding BiCGSTAB overlay is unsafe: \(\hat{\mathbf{p}}\) must survive past the mat-vec that produces \(\mathbf{v}\), all the way to the \(\mathbf{u}\)-update (line 15). \(\hat{\mathbf{p}}\) and \(\mathbf{v}\) require distinct storage, as do \(\hat{\mathbf{s}}\) and \(\mathbf{t}\). The only safe merge is \(\mathbf{s}\) overwriting \(\mathbf{r}\) in place (line 10), with \(\mathbf{r}_k\) then overwriting it again (line 16) — reflected in the 8-buffer count above.

8. Convergence, Breakdowns, and Restarts

8.1 Stopping criteria — inherited, with one adaptation

The companion §6 criteria transfer directly, measured on the same quantity \(\|\mathbf{R}^{T}\mathbf{r}_k\|/\|\hat{\mathbf{b}}\|\): the relative-residual threshold, the half-decade-improvement rule, the minimum-iteration floor with its \(10^{-15}\) bypass, and the absolute-tolerance mode. Two adaptations:

  • The divergence guard must be loosened. CG's "abort if the residual more than doubles" is calibrated to a method whose preconditioned-energy error is monotone. BiCGSTAB residual histories legitimately spike by orders of magnitude before converging. Proposed guard: abort only when the residual exceeds a large multiple (default \(10^{4}\)) of the best residual seen so far, or when it is non-finite. Anything tighter converts normal BiCGSTAB behaviour into spurious failures.
  • Acceptance requires a true-residual check. The recursively updated \(\mathbf{r}_k\) can drift from \(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{u}_k\) in finite precision, especially across residual spikes — and the cheap norm of A.2 adds a cancellation caveat of its own. Before declaring convergence: recompute \(\mathbf{r}=\tilde{\mathbf{b}}_p-\mathbf{S}_p\mathbf{u}_p\) locally (one mat-vec) and gather its norm (one gather round). Accept only if the true norm also passes; otherwise continue with the recomputed residual. Cost: one extra mat-vec + gather, once per solve in the common case.

8.2 Breakdown detection and restart

BiCGSTAB has three breakdown denominators, all detectable from scalars the schedule already reduces:

9. What This Fixes — and What It Does Not

FIXED 
  • Method validity. The recurrences are derived for general nonsingular \(\mathbf{A}\). The companion §7.3 warning — "classical PCG theory no longer applies" — is retired for this driver, replaced by the standard (weaker, but honest) BiCGSTAB theory: finite termination in exact arithmetic absent breakdown, no monotonicity.
  • The SPD preconditioner requirement disappears. Companion §7.4's warning that directed diagonal lumping does not preserve SPD threatened CG's formal validity. BiCGSTAB requires only that the hierarchical solve be nonsingular — which KLU verifies factor-by-factor and reports, mapping cleanly onto exit 4. Sparsification quality now affects only speed.
  • Failure semantics. Breakdowns are named, detected by scale-aware scalar tests, and answered by a principled restart with a provably non-degenerate fresh shadow — instead of a wrong-method iteration that may quietly stall inside generic guards.
NOT FIXED 
  • The bottom-level surrogate. BiCGSTAB converges to the solution of the stored system. Where the bottom-level reduction substituted \(\mathbf{B}^{T}\) for an independently stamped \(\mathbf{C}\) (companion §7.2), the converged answer is still the surrogate's answer. That is a modelling/storage decision upstream of any Krylov method.
  • No global convergence guarantee. For general matrices no short-recurrence method has one. BiCGSTAB can stagnate (notably on spectra with large imaginary parts — the BiCGSTAB(ℓ) escalation of Section 2 is the standard remedy) and, in exact arithmetic, can break down; the restart machinery bounds but does not eliminate this.
  • Preconditioner quality still rules. The sparsified hierarchical solve determines the iteration count exactly as before; a poor preconditioner is now a slow solve rather than an invalid one, but it is still slow.
  • The symmetric path should keep CG. On the default SPD flow, CG is strictly better: half the preconditioner solves and mat-vecs per iteration, one communication round instead of three, monotone error, full theory. BiCGSTAB is the fallback for the general path, not a replacement.

A.1 Dot-product rules in the duplicated space

Rule 1 (inherited). For consistent \(\mathbf{y}\) (duplicated as \(\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{R}\hat{\mathbf{y}}\)) and distributed \(\mathbf{x}\):

\[ \sum_p \mathbf{y}_p^{T}\mathbf{x}_p =(\mathbf{R}\hat{\mathbf{y}})^{T}\mathbf{x} =\hat{\mathbf{y}}^{T}(\mathbf{R}^{T}\mathbf{x}) =\hat{\mathbf{y}}^{T}\hat{\mathbf{x}} \]

— the consistent factor carries the same value into every copy, so summing over copies performs the gather on the distributed factor for free (companion A.1). BiCGSTAB uses this for \(\hat{\mathbf{r}}_0^{T}\mathbf{r}\), \(\hat{\mathbf{r}}_0^{T}\mathbf{v}\), \(\hat{\mathbf{r}}_0^{T}\mathbf{s}\), \(\hat{\mathbf{r}}_0^{T}\mathbf{t}\) — which is precisely why \(\hat{\mathbf{r}}_0\) is constructed consistent (§4.2).

Rule 2 (new). For two distributed vectors the true product is \(\hat{\mathbf{t}}^{T}\hat{\mathbf{s}} =(\mathbf{R}^{T}\mathbf{t})^{T}(\mathbf{R}^{T}\mathbf{s})\), and \(\sum_p\mathbf{t}_p^{T}\mathbf{s}_p\) differs from it by every cross-owner term \(\sum_{p\neq q}\mathbf{t}_p^{T}\mathbf{s}_q\) at shared nodes (the \(0.3/0.7\) example of §4.1). The only correct evaluation gathers at least one factor. Gathering both and dotting the unique fully-summed slots (Figure 2) is what the schedule does — each unique node contributes exactly once, so the slice sum is the assembled product. ✓

Type preservation. Every update in the loop preserves the table of §4: line 6 combines distributed with distributed (\(\mathbf{p}\) stays distributed); line 15 combines consistent with consistent (\(\mathbf{u}\) stays consistent); lines 10 and 16 combine distributed with distributed. The two \(\mathbf{M}^{-1}\) applications and the two \(\mathbf{A}\) applications are the only type conversions, in opposite directions, twice per iteration (Figure 1). The types never leak — the same closure argument as companion A.1.

A.2 The fused-scalar identities and their numerical caveat

Next iteration's \(\rho\) for free. By linearity of Rule 1 in the distributed factor:

\[ \rho_{k+1} =\hat{\mathbf{r}}_0^{T}\mathbf{r}_k =\hat{\mathbf{r}}_0^{T}(\mathbf{s}-\omega_k\mathbf{t}) =\underbrace{\hat{\mathbf{r}}_0^{T}\mathbf{s}}_{\text{local, batched}} -\;\omega_k\,\underbrace{\hat{\mathbf{r}}_0^{T}\mathbf{t}}_{\text{local, batched}} \]

Both partials are consistent × distributed, computed locally before \(\omega_k\) is known, and shipped in reduction #2. Line 4 of the next iteration therefore costs nothing — which is what holds the loop at two reductions.

The residual norm for free. \(\omega_k\) is the least-squares minimiser of \(\|\mathbf{s}_g-\omega\,\mathbf{t}_g\|\), so the minimised residual is orthogonal to \(\mathbf{t}_g\) and

\[ \|\mathbf{R}^{T}\mathbf{r}_k\|^2 =\|\mathbf{s}_g\|^2-\frac{(\mathbf{t}_g^{T}\mathbf{s}_g)^2}{\mathbf{t}_g^{T}\mathbf{t}_g}, \qquad \omega_k=\frac{\mathbf{t}_g^{T}\mathbf{s}_g}{\mathbf{t}_g^{T}\mathbf{t}_g}, \]

with \(\|\mathbf{s}_g\|\) already returned by preconditioner solve #2's down-sweep. The convergence norm therefore needs no gather of \(\mathbf{r}_k\) at all.

Cancellation caveat. The norm identity subtracts nearly equal quantities exactly when the stabilisation step is most effective (\(\|\mathbf{r}_k\|\ll\|\mathbf{s}\|\)) — the computed value can lose most of its significant digits at the moment it matters. This is why Section 8.1's acceptance test recomputes the true residual (one mat-vec, one gather) before declaring convergence, and why the identity's value is treated as a trigger for that test rather than as the final word. The same check catches recursive-residual drift across BiCGSTAB's residual spikes.
